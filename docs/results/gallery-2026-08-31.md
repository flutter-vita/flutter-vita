# The gallery on both renderers, and what worst-frame was hiding

First full `tools/bench.sh` run since the POLLOUT fix. Both renderers, 18
scenes. The raw results are in `gallery-2026-08-31/`.

Two things came out of it: a list of what is actually slow, and the discovery
that the gate had been grading the one metric that cannot be trusted on half
the scenes.

## The worst scenes on the GPU path, which is the default

| scene | worst frame | stall | build | raster |
|-------|------------:|------:|------:|-------:|
| `list_churn` | 283 ms | **2,667 ms** | **92.5** | 8.7 |
| `blur_backdrop` | 100 ms | **2,100 ms** | 10.9 | **41.5** |
| `route_hero` | 150 ms | 617 ms | 1.3 | 2.7 |
| `route_push` | 150 ms | 217 ms | 1.1 | 6.4 |
| `route_text_uniq` | 133 ms | 200 ms | 1.1 | 1.2 |

`list_churn` is the worst animation on the console and it is not the renderer's
fault: 92.5 ms of build against 8.7 ms of raster. `blur_backdrop` is the
opposite and the only scene where the rasteriser is the constraint.

The route scenes are neither, and that is the interesting row.

## Route transitions: 60 fps with one 133 ms frame in it

Every `route_*` scene reports median 16.67 ms and p95 16.67 ms. They run at a
clean 60. What the worst-frame column is reporting is **one outlier frame**,
always at or near 133.33 ms, which is exactly eight vsyncs.

Pulling those frames apart:

| scene | period | build | raster | unaccounted |
|-------|-------:|------:|-------:|------------:|
| `route_boxes` | 133.33 | 28.5 | 6.8 | 98 |
| `route_spans` | 133.33 | 55.1 | 11.9 | 66 |
| `route_bare` | 133.33 | 4.2 | 39.3 | 85 |
| `route_wrap` (baseline) | 133.33 | 27.3 | 35.8 | 70 |

Between half and three quarters of that frame is in neither Dart build nor
rasterisation, and the totals are inconsistent between scenes while the period
is not. A recurring ~100 ms that belongs to no phase, at irregular intervals, on
scenes doing very little, is the shape of a collector pause. Untested.

`route_boxes` in the baseline puts its spikes at frames 7, 46, 85, 124, 163,
202, 241 and 280 — every 39 frames exactly, which is the scene's own route-push
cadence and costs 33 ms. The 133 ms frame is a different thing that lands once.

## Software against GPU

Where the GPU wins, it wins enormously:

| scene | software | GPU | |
|-------|---------:|----:|---|
| `blur_backdrop` | 1,800 ms | 100 ms | 18.0x |
| `route_text_uniq` | 2,183 | 133 | 16.4x |
| `route_push`, `route_hero` | 2,183 | 150 | 14.6x |
| `image_scale` | 183 | 17 | 11.0x |
| `save_layer_tint` | 433 | 67 | 6.5x |
| `text_slide` | 500 | 83 | 6.0x |

The scenes where software appears to win — `route_spans` and `route_boxes` at
0.38x — are the outlier above, landing inside the GPU window and outside the
software one. There is no scene where software is genuinely faster.

## What the gate was doing wrong

It graded `worst_ms` and nothing else. On the route scenes that is a single
frame whose presence in the sample window is a coin flip, so the same code
grades 33.3 one run and 133.3 the next. This report produced three wrong answers
from it at once:

- `route_spans` and `route_boxes` flagged as +300% regressions. They are not.
- `route_wrap` silently "improved" 8x, 133.3 to 16.7, by the same mechanism.
- **`blur_backdrop` passed** while its raster mean went 8.0 ms to 41.5 ms and
  its stall doubled to 2.1 s — because its worst frame came in at 100 ms against
  a baseline of 133 ms, an improvement.

So `tools/bench-report.py` now also grades `raster_ms` and `stall_ms`, both of
which are means or totals across the whole window and cannot flip on where one
outlier fell. Wider margin, 25%, with an absolute floor on each so a mean moving
0.5 ms to 0.7 ms is not news.

Grading more metrics immediately produced two false alarms of its own — sw
`blur_backdrop` at +492% and sw `image_scale` at +94% — both from scenes the
table already refuses to print a number for, because they managed four and eight
frames. The gate now skips any scene that fails the same span-and-frames test
the table uses, on either side of the comparison.

## The real regressions, after all that

| | scene | metric | was | now | |
|---|-------|--------|----:|----:|---|
| sw | `save_layer_tint` | raster | 40.1 ms | 414.4 ms | **+934%** |
| gl | `blur_backdrop` | raster | 8.0 ms | 41.5 ms | **+421%** |
| gl | `blur_backdrop` | stall | 1,117 ms | 2,100 ms | +88% |
| sw | `text_slide` | worst | 216.7 ms | 500.0 ms | +131% |
| sw | `text_slide` | stall | 1,100 ms | 2,033 ms | +85% |
| sw | `opacity_fade` | stall | 550 ms | 1,067 ms | +94% |
| gl | `route_push` | raster | 3.0 ms | 6.4 ms | +110% |

`save_layer_tint` on software at ten times its baseline raster, and
`blur_backdrop` at four times on the GPU path, are the two worth chasing. Both
are compositing operations and both moved in the same direction on different
renderers, which argues for something above the renderer rather than in either.

`text_slide` is the known hinting regression and it is worse than the 2.31x it
was recorded at.

**The baseline was deliberately not rewritten.** Doing that here would erase
exactly the rows above.
