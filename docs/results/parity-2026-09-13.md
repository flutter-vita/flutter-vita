# vitaGL and fvita_gles2 on the same engine, scene by scene

**Question:** M4 of ADR-0019 asks for the bench at parity. Against what? The
committed baseline is from 2026-08-25, before the 444 MHz clocks and before
patch 0012. This is the first pair of runs on the same engine, the same day
(2026-09-12 evening for fvita_gles2, 2026-09-13 morning for vitaGL), the same
gallery source.

**Builds:** `build-vita-app.sh apps/gallery --gl vitagl` and `--gl gles2`,
engine with patches 0001 to 0012. Results kept as
`out/bench/gl-vitagl-0012-2026-09-13.json` and
`out/bench/gl-gles2-0012-2026-09-12.json`; the fvita_gles2 run had no shader
cache yet.

The user's own reading of the console the same evening: transitions run
smoother on fvita_gles2 than under vitaGL, and the list that sticks stuck
under vitaGL too.

## What it says

**Raster time per frame** is equal or lower on fvita_gles2 for 24 of 36
scenes and lower in total (185 vs 221 ms). `blur_backdrop` goes from 42.1 to
7.0 ms and its stall from 1050 to 233 ms; `save_layer_tint` loses its 617 ms
stall. Five scenes are higher by the bench's own rule (25% and 1 ms), all of
them route pushes: `route_bare` 1.0 → 3.7, `route_wrap` 5.3 → 9.1,
`route_spans` 1.0 → 3.2, `route_grid_bare` 1.1 → 4.8, `route_push` 1.1 → 3.4.
Something the layer does per frame of a page transition costs 2.5 to 3.8 ms
that vitaGL does not pay. Not yet found; the suspects are the fragment
program patched per blend state, the uniform upload, and the target switch
for each saveLayer.

**Worst frames of exactly 500 ms** on eight scenes are one thing: the first
use of a program, compiled by `libshacccg` on the raster thread (150 ms to
1.9 s each, 31 programs in the suite). vitaGL keeps its programs on disk in
`ux0:data/shader_cache`; this run of fvita_gles2 had no cache. Those scenes
are also the stall regressions: `clip_rrect`, `transform_rotate`,
`text_slide`, `layout_resize`, `shader_mask`, `spinner`. The cache is in the
next build and is measured in the follow-up below.

**The UI-thread stalls are the same on both.** `list_churn` 3.25 s under
vitaGL, 3.9 s under fvita_gles2, median frame 150 ms on both; `scroll_fling`
883 vs 867 ms; `route_hero` 717 vs 700 ms. The bench attributes them to the
UI thread on both backends. They are not a backend property and do not count
against parity; finding their source is its own item.

**`heap=`** under vitaGL stayed at 8 to 9 MB through all 34 scenes. On
2026-09-01 the same suite reached 158 MB and was killed by the kernel. Patch
0012 closed that on both backends.

The eight 500 ms worst frames are gone from the scenes that compiled:
`save_layer_tint` 500 → 100, `clip_rrect` 500 → 100, `transform_rotate`
500 → 100, `text_slide` 500 → 83, `shader_mask` 533 → 117, `spinner`
533 → 33. Worst-frame sum 9633 → 5917 ms (vitaGL 4450), stall sum
14300 → 11900 ms (vitaGL 10750). The two compiles this run still paid for
landed in `transform_scale` (533 ms) and in `blur_backdrop`: the blur's
fragment shader is generated per kernel, so its text differs between runs
(fs 903 B one run, 637 B the next) and the cache fills over several launches,
as vitaGL's does.

What the cache does not change is per-frame cost, and two gaps remain there:

- **`blur_backdrop`: 110 to 130 ms per steady frame on this layer, 42 ms under
  vitaGL.** The bench's `raster` column hid it in the first run (7.0 ms, a
  sample window that landed outside the blur) and shows it in the second
  (120 ms). The app log has it in both: `JANK frame ... raster 109..130 ms`,
  frame after frame, in both runs. The backdrop copy is `glCopyTexSubImage2D`,
  which this layer does as a `sceGxmFinish` and a CPU copy of the region,
  texel by texel, then Skia's downsample passes each switch the render target.
  A GPU-side copy (the display buffer wrapped as a strided texture, drawn into
  the destination) removes the wait and the CPU loop; it is the next item.
- **Route pushes: 2 to 8 ms per frame more than vitaGL** (`route_bare` 1.0 vs
  5.7, `route_grid_bare` 1.1 vs 9.5, `route_push` 1.1 vs 3.1), with high
  variance between runs. Not yet attributed.

The UI-thread numbers moved on their own between the two fvita_gles2 runs
(`scroll_fling` stall 867 → 1667 ms, `fill_lerp` worst 33 → 250 ms) with
nothing in the renderer changed between them, which is the noise floor for
anything the bench blames on the UI thread.

## Follow-up, later the same morning: the GPU copy and tiled render targets

Two changes to the layer, each measured on the console before the next:

1. **`glCopyTexSubImage2D` on the GPU** (`gles2/src/blit.c`): the source's
   scene ends, a scene on the destination draws a quad sampling the source,
   the frame goes on. No `sceGxmFinish`, no CPU loop. `blur_backdrop` raster
   went from 110 to 130 ms a frame to 24.6 (vitaGL 42.1).

2. **Render targets in the tiled layout.** The stats line gained timers, and
   they said where the route-scene gap was: the time inside the swap was
   `sceGxmDisplayQueueAddEntry` blocking, 4 to 7 ms a frame on `route_bare`,
   because the GPU's latency per frame (measured from the display callback)
   was 18 to 22 ms during a page push. The GPU was sampling full-screen linear
   surfaces slowly. A texture now becomes 32 by 32 tiled the first time it is
   drawn into; the CPU paths on it go through a linear staging texture and a
   blit. GPU latency on `route_bare` 14 to 15 ms, swap wait 0.4 to 2.5 ms.

## Follow-up, the afternoon: the same runtime, the freeze fixed

The lines above were taken with the Dart runtime's trace writes on
(`docs/results/ui-thread-list-churn-2026-09-13.md`) and with a condition
variable that lost wakeups (`docs/results/the-frozen-ui-thread-2026-09-13.md`).
Both backends, build 23 of the gallery, same engine, same hour, 36 scenes,
no scene interrupted on either:

Raster equal or lower on fvita_gles2 on 20 of 36 scenes. The scenes where
it is higher by more than a millisecond are the route pushes with the Zoom
transition (`route_wrap` 1.1 against 4.9, `route_spans` 0.9 against 6.2,
`route_grid_bare` 5.1 against 11.8, `route_tx_zoom_nosnap` 9.2 against

(4.2 against 6.8). `blur_backdrop` is 42.6 against 27.3 the other way, and
`alpha_probe` 9.4 against 1.1.

Run-to-run spread on one backend is about as wide as the gap between them:
fvita_gles2 builds 21, 22 and 23 on the same code path summed stall at 13850,
17267 and 7650 ms, the first two with the lock wrappers' cost in them. One
pair of runs settles the order of magnitude and the Zoom gap; it does not
settle a 10% difference.
